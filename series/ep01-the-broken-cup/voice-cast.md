# Voice Cast & Direction — EP01: The Broken Cup

*Keyed line-by-line to `script.md` (471 lines, 114 dialogue turns). Cue numbers run 1–114 in script order and are the naming key for the voice session.*

**A voice tool has since been picked — see `TOOL-DECISIONS.md` §1 — but this document stays deliberately tool-agnostic**, and that is now a feature rather than a gap. Everything below is performance direction that holds whether a line is performed and converted, performed and used raw, or read by a hired voice actor. No product, model, or vendor is named anywhere in this file, so a tool change costs nothing here.

One thing the decision does change, and it changes how you read every direction below: **the method is speech-to-speech, not text-to-speech.** Every line of both characters is performed into a microphone and then converted; Gramps Lou ships as the creator's own voice. So the timing, breath, volume and melody notes in §3 and §4 are instructions to a *performer* — you — not prompts for a model. The matched pairs in §4 are recordable back to back in one sitting for the same reason. The "When a tool is picked" checklist in §6 is what gets filled in the day the pre-flight test passes.

**Two speaking voices in this episode. That is the whole cast.** Maya and Gramps Lou. No Theo, no Nell, no Ms. Rivera, no one-off side character — the conflict only works if Maya is genuinely the only one who was in the room. **No narrator, no host, no announcer, no voice-over of any kind.** The end card is on-screen text and is **not read aloud**.

### Perspective / mic-distance rule

The camera is static wide the whole episode and **never cuts in**, so the voice must not cut in either. Hold one consistent recording perspective (distance, room, tone colour) from frame one to the end card, with exactly **two** deliberate exceptions:

1. **Cue 4** — Gramps Lou is off-frame behind the stand, over a running saw. Perform with real distance and projection; let the mix do the rest. Do not perform it close and filter it into the distance afterwards.
2. **Cue 68** — Maya is flat on her stomach with her head under the bench. She is **loud and obstructed**, not quiet. Perform the obstruction; do not fake it by dropping volume.

Everything else is the same room, the same distance, the same size — including the Closing. The Closing is not a booth-forward "address the audience" perspective; she stays exactly where she has been all episode.

## 2. Global delivery rules

1. **Underplay everything.** This is a low-anxiety show. Nothing in this episode is pushed — not the break, not the lie, not the confession, not the lesson.
2. **Silence is written content, not dead air.** 1:19 of the 6:38 runtime is wordless by design. Deliver per-line stems and let the edit place the gaps. **Never let a tool auto-trim leading/trailing silence, and never let a performer fill a pause with "um," a breath-catch, or a sound of thinking.**
3. **No line is a lesson.** If a read starts sounding like it knows it is the important one, it is wrong.
4. **Gramps Lou never catches her.** Not once. No suspicion, no detective colour, no knowing pause, no reverse psychology. He believes "it just fell" and he acts on it — that is what makes the failed try fail.
5. **He also never lectures.** His longest line is 11 words. Every line of his is a question, a short answer, or a one-line instruction. There is no monologue to find and none should be invented in the read.
6. **Two "It just fell"s and one "It didn't just fall" must share a melody** (cues 10, 36, 51). Record them in the same session, back to back, then place them.
7. **Nobody is laughed at.** No line is ever pointed at Maya, including her own.
8. **Grade 2–3, so speak it like speech.** Real contractions, no over-articulation, no read-aloud diction, no "storytime" consonants.
9. **Record every line, including trim candidates.** Lines marked † are on the script's trim list — they get cut in the edit if the runtime needs it, never in the booth.
10. **Protect the Shorts moment.** Cues 9 → pause → 10 → 11 are the Shorts cut. Deliver those four as one clean, isolated, unprocessed set so the Shorts edit never needs a re-render.

## 5. What must NOT be performed

Any of these in a take means the take is unusable, not fixable in the mix.

**Never, for Maya:**
- **No baby-talk.** No dropped consonants for cuteness, no lisp-for-charm, no "widdle" anything. She is seven and she talks like it, which is clearly and quickly.
- **No crying that reads as distressing.** She never cries in this script and she must not cry in the booth. No sobs, no sniffs, no wobble that tips into tears, no catch in the throat. Her lowest moments (cues 49, 55) are **thinned and quiet**, and that is the whole permitted range. A child viewer must never feel bad watching her.
- **No panic in Scene 1.** Small kid-sized alarm, not fear. No scream, no horror gasp, no hyperventilating.
- **No "kid voice" mugging** — the adult-doing-a-child performance with the squeezed throat and the swooping pitch. If a grown performer or a synthetic voice is used, it must still read as a real seven-year-old and never as an impression of one.
- **No sing-song.** Particularly on cue 18 ("Big, middle, small") and on the Closing.
- **No presenter energy on cue 113.** No upswing cadence, no "hey friends," no widening smile. The second she sounds like she is hosting, the closing becomes a lecture and the show's core rule breaks.
- **No pride or triumph on the skill line (110).** Not delivered, not discovered, not landed. Said.
- **No sarcasm.** She is seven and she is not equipped with it.

**Never, for Gramps Lou:**
- **No adult sarcasm.** Not one dry aside, not one arched word. He has a dry *timbre*, which is not the same as a dry *attitude*.
- **No suspicion or detective colour.** Cues 12, 32, 34, 39 are all easy to poison with a knowing tone. He never catches her, never sets a trap, never tests her. Cue 48 in particular must be entirely sincere.
- **No lecturing, sermonising, or "wise elder" cadence.** No slowing down to make a point, no weight dropped onto a key word, no pause-for-effect before an answer. He has no monologue; the read must not manufacture one.
- **No disappointment.** No sighs, no "oh, Maya," no head-shake in the voice. He is a little mad about the cup and not at her, exactly as written.
- **No cartoon-grandpa.** No wheeze, no quaver, no doddering, no gravel-croak, no hard-of-hearing beat. He is capable, working, and steady. He is not anyone's frail grandfather and he is not necessarily Maya's grandfather at all.
- **No raised voice, ever.** Not once in the series.
- **No warmth pushed on top of cue 59 ("Not at you").** Sweetening it kills it.

**Never, for either:**
- **No narrator, no host, no voice-over, no announcer, no fourth-wall line except cues 113–114.** Nothing reads the end card.
- **No laughing at the other character.** Nobody is the joke.
- **No filled pauses.** No "um," no "uh," no performed thinking noises anywhere near the Scene 2 or Scene 4 holds.
- **No accent, age, or energy drift between scenes or sessions.** One voice per character for twelve episodes.
- **No ad-libs, no paraphrase, no added "yeah"s or "okay"s.** The word count is budgeted to the second; extra words break the runtime table.
- **No scary sound in any performance** — no shouting, no sudden dynamic spikes. This is a low-anxiety watch, every time.

## 6. When a tool is picked — settings to lock and save

Fill this in **once**, the day the voice decision is made, and save it to a single permanent file (suggested: `/voice/voice-lock.md`) alongside the reference takes. Then update the tool table in `PRODUCTION-SOP.md` and close the voice gap in `CONTEXT.md`. **The purpose of every item below is that episode 12 sounds like episode 1.**

### Lock and write down — both paths

- [ ] **Character-to-voice map**, written out: which voice is Maya, which is Gramps Lou. One voice per character, for the life of the series.
- [ ] **Reference take per character** — the canonical tuning-fork clip every future session is matched against. Use **cues 9–11** for the pair (his open question, her lie) and **cues 109–110** for Maya alone. Store the audio, not a description of it.
- [ ] **Record reference takes for the characters who are NOT in this episode** — Theo and Nell — in the same first session, before anything else drifts. Both are locked in `/characters/`, and Theo arrives as soon as EP02.
- [ ] **Ms. Rivera is the exception — no reference take yet.** She is recurring per BIBLE.md but has **no locked sheet in `/characters/`** (see `character-sheet.md`), so there is no age, personality, or description to direct a take against, and whatever got recorded would become her lock by default. Lock her sheet in `/characters/` first; record her reference take in the session before her first school-set episode.
- [ ] **Pronunciation list**, locked now: Maya · Gramps Lou · Maple Row · Theo · Nell · Ms. Rivera · Ridgeline Elementary. Note the stress pattern for each, and whether "Gramps Lou" is ever shortened (it is not).
- [ ] **Audio format**, identical every episode: sample rate, bit depth, mono/stereo, file type. Suggested default if nothing else is pulling you: 48 kHz / 24-bit / mono / WAV.
- [ ] **Loudness targets**, written as numbers: dialogue stems and the final mix. Suggested default: final mix −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP.
- [ ] **Delivery format: one file per cue, not one file per scene.** Named `ep01_c010_maya.wav` (cue numbers in this document). Per-line stems are what lets the edit place the 1:19 of written silence.
- [ ] **Silence handling: auto-trim OFF.** Wherever the chosen path has a "strip silence," "trim ends," or "tighten" behaviour, turn it off and note where the switch lives. The pauses are content.
- [ ] **The non-verbal assets** `nv01`–`nv07` are ordered explicitly as separate deliverables in every session, not assumed.
- [ ] **Turnaround and cost per episode**, measured on EP01 and recorded, so the weekly schedule in `90-DAY-PLAN.md` is based on a real number.
- [ ] **Rights**: commercial use plus monetisation is permitted for a Made for Kids channel, in writing. Confirm nothing in the chosen path is a likeness of a real person without consent.
- [ ] **Re-test protocol**: before accepting any upgrade, version change, new performer, new room, or new setting, re-record the reference cues and A/B them against the stored EP01 takes. If they do not match, do not upgrade mid-season.

### If the path is a synthetic voice

- [ ] **Voice identity and engine version pinned and written down** — the exact voice identifier *and* the exact model/engine version. Turn off anything that auto-upgrades the model underneath you.
- [ ] **Every generation parameter recorded as a number**, not a memory: whatever the chosen system calls stability/consistency, similarity, expressiveness/style, and speed. Save the settings file or a screenshot next to the reference takes.
- [ ] **Seed or determinism setting** captured, if the system has one.
- [ ] **The exact input text is the script text** — no added stage directions inside the spoken string unless the system reads them as direction, and if it does, record which bracket/tag syntax was used so it is reproducible.
- [ ] **Per-line generation, not per-scene**, so retakes are cheap and silence stays in the editor's hands.
- [ ] **Keep the top three takes per cue**, not just the chosen one — retaining alternates is much cheaper than regenerating after a version change.
- [ ] **Check the two perspective cues by ear** (4 and 68). Synthetic voices handle distance and obstruction badly; plan to perform or process them deliberately.
- [ ] **Listen specifically for the forbidden colours** — sing-song on the list, presenter lift on the closing, knowing/smug colour on Gramps Lou. These are the default failure modes of generated reads and they will not announce themselves.

### If the path is real voices

- [ ] **Signed release covering commercial use and monetisation**, plus continued availability for a weekly series — a voice that disappears at episode 6 is a re-cast of the whole show.
- [ ] **If Maya is cast with a child performer**: guardian consent on file, any required work permit, short sessions with real breaks, and an explicit brief that **no crying is wanted** in any take.
- [ ] **Recording chain written down**: mic model, polar pattern, distance, height, pop filter, gain, interface, room, treatment, and headphone mix. Photograph the setup.
- [ ] **A 30-second calibration read at the top of every session**, matched against the stored EP01 reference take before any new lines are recorded.
- [ ] **Same room, same time of day where possible.** A voice at 8am and a voice at 9pm are two different voices.
- [ ] **Both characters in the same session where the matched pairs in §4 are concerned**, or record Maya's side to playback of his.
- [ ] **Room tone captured every session** — 30 seconds minimum. The written silence has to sound like the same room it came from.
- [ ] **Processing chain saved as a preset** (EQ, compression, de-esser, noise reduction), applied identically to every episode.

### Do not close the gap halfway

Per `PRODUCTION-SOP.md`, **two** gaps block Week 1 production: voice and editing. Picking a voice path closes one. Nothing publishes until both are chosen — script and visual-prompt work can keep moving in the meantime, and this file is written so that it does not need rewriting when the decision lands.
