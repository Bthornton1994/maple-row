**Content**
- [ ] Script matches the episode formula (hook → problem → failed try → new try → skill named → practice)
  - Hook: frame one is the blue-striped mug in four pieces on the concrete, one piece against Maya's untied shoe, Maya still up on the crate with her hand out. No fade-in, no title card, no establishing shot, no logo first.
  - Problem: Gramps Lou's "What happened?" in Scene 2, and the fact that nobody saw it.
  - Failed try: "It just fell. All by itself." propped up with "Maybe the wind did it" — and it fails by what it costs her (wrong bolt jars, warm peach, the refused pencil) and by sending him to build a lip on a shelf that was never broken. Nobody catches her.
  - New try: Scene 4, "Wait!" / "Don't fix the shelf" with the drill bit already on good wood, then "I dropped it. It was me."
  - Skill named: Scene 6, by Maya, one plain sentence — *"Telling the truth was hard for one minute, but the lie was hard all day."* Gramps Lou nods once and adds nothing.
  - Practice: the Closing, 40 words / 0:22, straight from the backlog's `practice_for_viewer`.
  - No narrator, no host. The only look at camera in the whole episode is Maya's in the Closing, and it plays as an invitation.
- [ ] No hard-rule violations (CONTEXT.md): no horror/cringe/politics, no fake-educational excuse, no medical/legal "instead of a parent" advice
  - No villain, no cliffhanger, no scares, no scary sound design under the break or the pause.
  - No magic, gadget, or lucky break: the handle is found because she said which way the cup went, the first glue press fails and has to be redone, and the cup takes two real hours in the sun.
  - Nobody is laughed at or humiliated: Gramps Lou never catches her, never sets a trap, never raises his voice — "Or you want to keep sorting. Both are fine with me."
  - No adult lecture or monologue: his longest line is eleven words; every line is a question, a short answer, or a one-line instruction.
  - The practice is "say one small true thing out loud" — no claim to teach, train, or fix anything, and nothing that routes a kid around a grown-up.
- [ ] Every character matches their locked look from `/characters/`
  - Maya (`hero.md`): curly dark brown hair in two short puffs, warm brown skin, round face, gap-tooth smile, freckles across the nose, yellow t-shirt, teal overalls, one shoe slightly untied. Locked prompt paragraph copied verbatim into every frame.
  - Gramps Lou (`adult.md`): gray beard, flat cap, denim work apron over rolled-up plaid sleeves, deep brown skin, and a visible physical task in every scene he is in — including the Closing, where he is still sanding at the frame edge.
  - Temporary marks are marks, not redesigns: the peach's wet ring on the overalls from Scene 3, and the dust stripe on her cheek in Scene 5, scrubbed off with her sleeve in the same shot and clean from there on.
  - The nose scrunch fires exactly once — Scene 2, in the last beat before "It just fell" — and nowhere else. The Shorts cut reuses that same beat; it does not get a second one. It never appears on the thumbnail.
  - Two characters only. No Theo, no Nell, no Sam, no one-off side character, and no incidental person anywhere in the plate: Maya has to be the only one who was in the room.
- [ ] Mistake gets a visible, concrete repair — not just an apology and a cut
  - On screen: five pieces lined up biggest to smallest, every edge dry-brushed, a thin line of glue, the first press failing ("It moved!"), the second press held twenty seconds, the band looped twice, two hours in the sun, the band coming off, and the cup standing on its own.
  - Every seam reads as a pale line, the handle sits a hair crooked, and it holds pencils instead of tea. The last frame of the episode is that seam.
  - The shelf in Scene 6 is exactly as it was: no lip, no screws, no holes, no pencil marks — he rubbed the two dots off with his thumb in Scene 4.
