## Video description (published copy)

This is the complete text for the description box. Nothing else goes in it — no URLs, no playlist links, no timestamps, no subscribe ask, no next-episode tease. It is addressed to the parent about the child, never to the child.

> Maya breaks Gramps Lou's favorite cup at his fix-it stand, says it fell by itself, and then has to watch him start fixing a shelf that was never broken. The skill is telling the truth: she owns up, finds the missing handle, and helps glue the cup back together — it holds pencils now instead of tea, and every seam still shows. It's a calm 6-minute watch with no scares and no cliffhanger, and it ends on a 20-second "your turn": pick one small true thing to say out loud today, even if it's a little embarrassing.

Three sentences. Sentence 1 is what happens, sentence 2 is the skill and the concrete repair, sentence 3 is tone, runtime, and the exact practice from the backlog's `practice_for_viewer` field. A parent who reads only sentence 1 already knows what their kid is about to watch.

**Deliberately included:** the fact that the cup is visibly mended and imperfect ("every seam still shows"). That is the episode's whole position on mistakes, and it is the detail that separates this from a be-nice moral.

**Deliberately left out:** anything that reads as a surprise, a hook, or a tease. No "and you won't believe what she does next." No emotional stakes language. No claim that the video teaches, trains, or improves anything measurable.

## Thumbnail

**One clear face + one object + 2–4 words.** Nothing else in frame.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Face** | Maya, alone. The Scene 2 pause: mouth closed in a flat line, eyebrows up, eyes looking up and off to one side — toward the shelf, not at camera. Worried but steady. Locked look per `/characters/hero.md` (two short curly puffs, yellow t-shirt, teal overalls). **Not** crying, not scared, not comic. **Not** the nose scrunch — that tell fires exactly once, inside the episode, and it belongs to the Shorts cut. |
| **Object** | The broken blue-striped mug, in pieces, held in both her hands at chest height below the face. One object, one hero prop, large enough to read at grid size. The blue stripe is the only marking on it — no lettering, no motif. |
| **Words** | **"I broke it."** — 3 words. |
| **Background** | The fix-it stand plate, simplified and low-contrast so the face and the cup carry. No second character — Gramps Lou is not in the thumbnail. No incidental people. Warm daytime palette. |

**Word choice.** "I broke it" is the true thing, in kid words, sitting right next to a picture of a broken cup — unambiguous for a parent scanning in half a second, and it matches both the skill and the practice.

- **Alternate worth an A/B test:** **"That was me."** — 3 words, and it is one of the two exact phrases Maya hands the viewer in the closing practice, so it ties the thumbnail to the takeaway. Slightly less legible next to the cup, since it does not name what happened.
- **Rejected:** **"It just fell."** Do not put the lie on the thumbnail. Without the clip around it, it reads as the show's own caption.

**Hard don'ts:** no arrows, no circles, no red rings, no shock faces, no channel logo taking up a corner, no more than four words, no URL or handle, no subscribe or comment prompt. Test it legible at 120px wide.
