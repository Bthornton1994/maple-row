# Shorts Cut — EP01: The Broken Cup

*Cut entirely from existing episode material. No new dialogue, no new frames, no new location. Every shot below is an in/out pair on `script.md` — the Short is an assembly, not a production.*

---

## Source moment (from backlog `shorts_hook` field)

> "The moment right before Maya decides what to say."

That moment is **the Scene 2 pause** — `script.md` lines 55–63, source **0:37–0:48**, key frame **4** (push-in 1 of 3): Gramps Lou asks "What happened?", Maya's mouth opens, her eyes go up to the clean ring in the dust and back down, her shoulders come up with the breath and stay up, her freckled nose scrunches, and then she says "It just fell."

**That pause is the anchor of this cut — the longest single shot in it, uncut, 9 seconds — but it is not the whole cut.** Per `YOUTUBE-KIDS-COMPLIANCE.md`, a Short here is "one real moment … not a cliffhanger," and a Short that ends on the lie *is* a cliffhanger: it shows a kid getting away with something and rolls the feed. So the cut runs through to Maya actually telling the truth in Scene 4 and Gramps Lou's answer, and closes on the mended cup. The pause is the hook; the truth is the payoff; both are in the 55 seconds.

**Why this moment:** it is the only beat in the episode that carries the whole skill by itself. A viewer who sees nothing else gets a complete, closed story — she broke it, nobody saw, she said it fell on its own, it sat on her all morning, she stopped him before he fixed the wrong thing, she said "it was me," and the grown-up said thank you and wasn't mad at her. Nothing is withheld to make you go watch the episode. The episode is there for the viewer who now wants the middle.

## Runtime

**Target: 0:55.** Cap 0:60, floor 0:30 per the template. Trim list at the bottom takes it to 0:46 if the platform cut needs to come in under 50s.

Shot durations below are timed at **~135 wpm plus ~0.5s per speaker handoff** — the natural-read figure the script's own speaker-handoff check uses. The 110 wpm figure in the script's runtime table is a *scene budget* with handoff padding already folded in; using it to time an individual 18-word line would under-book that line by two seconds.

The 9-second pause is **~16% of the Short and is not negotiable** — `script.md` "Never trimmed" lists it first ("That shot is the episode and it is the Shorts cut"), and `brief.md` says to protect the silence in the edit. It will feel long in a feed. That is the point of it, and the hook caption holds on screen through it so a sound-off viewer is never looking at dead air.

## Framing and format

The episode is static wide 16:9 with detail sized to read in the wide (the dust ring, the two pencil dots, the seams). **Cropping in to fill 9:16 would cut out the exact details the shots were composed around** — the shelf and the dust ring sit above Maya's head in every one of these frames.

- **Center the 16:9 frame in a 9:16 canvas** on a flat warm band pulled from the fix-it-stand palette. No blur-fill, no zoom, no auto-reframe, no pan-and-scan.
- Top band carries the hook caption. Bottom band carries the dialogue captions, kept clear of the Shorts UI safe area.
- Push-in 1 is a scale-up of frame 4 and still costs nothing — it reads *better* letterboxed, because the frame grows into the band.
- Static wide and slow push-in only, same as the episode. No added cuts inside a shot, no speed ramps, no zoom punches.

## Caption / on-screen text

Shorts are watched sound-off. Three text layers, nothing else on screen.

**1. Hook caption (the required line).** Up from cut 0:00, holds all the way through the pause, out at cut 0:18 as Maya says "It just fell."

> **Nobody saw her drop it. She told the truth anyway.**

Ten words, all of them owned by a 7-year-old, two short lines at Shorts scale. It states the resolution up front on purpose: it is the strongest possible guard against the feed reading this as "wait for it," it tells a scanning parent exactly what their kid is about to watch, and it is what keeps a sound-off viewer with us through 9 seconds of silence. It gives away the outcome, not the watch — the interest is in *how* she does it, which is what's on screen.

- **A/B alternate:** **"Nobody saw her break it."** Five words, more legible, stronger pull. Held as the B because it leans on withheld outcome, which is the thing the compliance doc tells us not to do. Only test it if retention on A is genuinely poor, and never with any added "wait for it" styling.
- **Rejected:** anything with "you won't believe," "wait for it," "watch what she does," an arrow, or a countdown.

**2. Dialogue captions.** Every spoken line burned in, bottom third, two lines maximum, high-contrast on the flat band, plain rounded sans. No speaker labels — the cut alternates one kid and one grown-up and the lines are unmistakable. Nothing on screen during the pause except the hook caption.

**3. "Later." card** on shot 5 — one word, top of frame, cut 0:20–0:23. Pure edit furniture so a sound-off viewer reads the time jump; the warm peach and the box in the dust ring won't carry that in three seconds on their own. This is the only text in the Short that isn't either dialogue or lifted from the episode's own end card.

**End card** (over shot 12, frame 18, cut 0:52–0:55). Two lines, Maple Row logo, warm daytime palette:

> **Your turn: say one small true thing out loud today.**
> *Full story on the channel.*

Line one is verbatim from the episode's own end card and is the backlog's `practice_for_viewer`. Line two is the CTA.
