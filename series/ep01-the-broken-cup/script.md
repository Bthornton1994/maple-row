# Script — EP01: The Broken Cup

*Rules: 6–8 min spoken · grade 2–3 reading level · no narrator · ends on the 20-second practice. See EPISODE-ENGINE.md for the full rule set.*

*Cast: Maya, Gramps Lou. One location plate: Gramps Lou's fix-it stand, one continuous morning into afternoon. Camera: static wide throughout, with three slow push-ins, all marked. No close-ups, no cuts inside a scene — where a small detail has to read, it is animated large enough to read in the wide.*

*Plate staging (build once, reuse all six scenes): the high shelf runs the whole length of the stand, above both the front counter and the workbench end, so the dust ring and the box stay in frame from every working area. The upside-down crate is the only thing Maya can reach the high shelf from. The bench sits on casters — one wheel is where the handle ends up.*

---

## SCENE 1 — GRAMPS LOU'S FIX-IT STAND, FRONT COUNTER, MORNING
*(the problem — on screen in frame one)*

[VISUAL: FIRST FRAME OF THE EPISODE. No fade-in, no establishing shot, no title card, no build-up. Static wide on the concrete floor of the fix-it stand: a blue-striped mug lying in four pieces. One curved piece has rocked to a stop against Maya's untied shoe. Maya is still up on the upside-down crate, one hand out toward the high shelf, holding nothing. The first sound in the episode is the last piece settling.]

[VISUAL: From behind the stand, out of frame, a saw keeps going — shh-shh, shh-shh. It does not stop. He didn't hear it.]

**MAYA:** Oh no.

**MAYA:** *(whisper)* No, no, no, no.

[VISUAL: She climbs down off the crate slowly, like the floor might break too. She crouches and fits the two biggest pieces together in her hands. They almost make a cup. Almost.]

[VISUAL: She stands the two pieces back up on the shelf, balanced against each other like a whole cup, and takes her hands away. They fall apart again. This is her fast fix, and it lasts one second.]

**MAYA:** Come on. Come on.

[VISUAL: The high shelf above her — a clean round ring in the dust where the cup stood all morning. Plant this ring clearly and animate it big enough to read in the wide. It comes back in every scene.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** *(out of frame, over the saw)* You okay out there?

[VISUAL: Maya's head snaps up. Her hands close around the pieces and go behind her back before she has decided anything at all.]

**MAYA:** Yep! Fine!

[VISUAL: The saw stops. In the quiet you can hear one last piece rocking on the concrete, and then settling.]

## SCENE 2 — FIX-IT STAND, THE SAME SPOT, ONE MINUTE LATER
*(the failed try — the pause, then the lie)*

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou comes around the corner of the stand with a sanded board under one arm and a sanding block in his hand. He stops. He looks at the floor.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Ah.

[VISUAL: He sets the board on the counter, crouches down, and turns one curved piece over in his fingers.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** That's my every-morning cup.

**MAYA:** I know.

**GRAMPS LOU:** What happened?

[VISUAL: **THE PAUSE. This is the whole episode in one shot — hold it longer than feels comfortable.** Static wide, no cut. Maya's mouth opens. Nothing comes out.]

[VISUAL: Her eyes go up to the shelf — to the clean ring in the dust. Back down to Gramps Lou. Then to the floor.]

[VISUAL: SLOW PUSH-IN ON MAYA (1 of 3). She breathes in, and her shoulders come up with the breath and stay up there.]

[VISUAL: Her freckled nose scrunches. **This is the only nose scrunch in the episode — do not use the tell anywhere else.** Hold one full beat after it. No music under this. This is the Shorts moment; protect the silence in the edit.]

**MAYA:** It just fell.

[VISUAL: Her shoulders come down on the sentence. Not like relief — like setting something heavy on the ground that she already knows she has to pick back up.]

**MAYA:** All by itself. I just heard it go.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Huh.

[VISUAL: He does not look up at her. He picks the pieces up one at a time into his palm. No detective face, no long stare. He is not going to catch her — that is the point.]

**MAYA:** Maybe the wind did it.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Maybe.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Well. It was a good cup.

[VISUAL: He tips the pieces off his palm into a small cardboard box and puts the box up on the high shelf, right inside the clean ring in the dust. It fits the ring almost exactly.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Handle's around here somewhere. It'll turn up.

[VISUAL: He picks his board back up and goes back to work. Maya stays where she is, looking up at the box. Hold. He has already let it go, and she hasn't.]

---

## SCENE 3 — FIX-IT STAND, THE WORKBENCH END, LATER THAT MORNING
*(what the lie costs her — and what it makes him start building)*

[VISUAL: A kitchen chair upside down on the bench with a cracked rung. Gramps Lou brushes white glue into the crack, slow and careful. Behind him, up on the shelf, the little cardboard box sits in the dust ring. Keep the box in frame for almost every shot of this scene.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Bolt jars are yours if you want 'em.

[VISUAL: Maya climbs onto the stool. Three jars in front of her — big, middle, small. This is her favorite job in the world. Normally she can pick up a bolt without even looking at it.]

[VISUAL: She isn't looking at the jars. She's looking at the shelf.]

**MAYA:** Big, middle, small.

[VISUAL: She drops three small bolts into the big jar. She doesn't notice.]

[VISUAL: On any other morning she'd have caught that before it left her hand, and then argued about it for five minutes. Today she doesn't look down once.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Band, please.

**MAYA:** Which one?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Fat red one.

[VISUAL: She hands him a wrench. She looks at her own hand like it belongs to somebody else, and then hands him the red rubber band.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Glue does the sticking.

**MAYA:** And the band?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Band does the holding.

**MAYA:** Both?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Or it comes apart again.

[VISUAL: He stretches the red band around the chair leg, over the glued crack, and lets it snap tight. He stands the chair up in the sun at the end of the bench.]

[VISUAL: He opens the cooler, takes out a peach, wipes it on his apron, and holds it out to her.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Cold one.

**MAYA:** Thanks.

[VISUAL: She takes it in both hands. She does not eat it.]

**MAYA:** Gramps Lou?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Mm?

[VISUAL: Hold. Her mouth is open. Her eyes go to the shelf, the box, the ring in the dust. This is the small echo of the Scene 2 pause — same shape, no nose scrunch this time.]

**MAYA:** Could you fix it? Your cup.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Maybe. Can't fix what I can't find, though.

[VISUAL: Maya's eyes travel along the floor and stop. She doesn't know where the handle went either — she was up on the crate, not watching it land.]

**MAYA:** Oh.

[VISUAL: LONG STATIC WIDE, then SLOW PUSH-IN (2 of 3) — time passing inside one unbroken shot, no cut. The bolt jars are still half sorted and still wrong. The peach has gone warm in her hands and left a wet ring on her overalls. She hasn't taken one bite. Gramps Lou hums at the far end of the bench, winding a cord around his elbow. Every few seconds Maya's eyes go back up to the shelf.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** You're quiet.

**MAYA:** I'm sorting.

**MAYA:** *(to herself, very small)* It just fell.

[VISUAL: She says it the way you test a step to see if it will hold you. It doesn't.]

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou stops winding the cord. He is looking up at the high shelf.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Been thinking about that shelf.

**MAYA:** The shelf?

**GRAMPS LOU:** A cup doesn't jump off a flat shelf. Must be sloping.

[VISUAL: Maya goes very still on the stool.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** I'll run a lip along the front.

[VISUAL: He pulls a thin strip of wood off the scrap pile and holds it along the front edge of the shelf, sighting down it. He takes the flat carpenter pencil from behind his ear and holds it out to her, blunt end first.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Want to mark the line? Yours come out straighter.

**MAYA:** No thanks.

[VISUAL: Marking lines is the other job Maya loves at this stand, and she has never once said no to it. Gramps Lou looks at the pencil, puts it back behind his ear, and marks the line himself. No comment, no raised eyebrow. Two saw strokes. He blows the sawdust off the strip.]

## SCENE 4 — FIX-IT STAND, THE WORKBENCH END, A LITTLE LATER
*(the new try)*

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou stands the cut strip against the front edge of the high shelf and makes two small pencil dots on the good wood, one at each end. He picks up the drill. Static wide — animate the drill and the two pencil dots big enough to read without cutting in.]

[VISUAL: The bit touches the shelf. Maya is off the stool before she knows she is standing.]

**MAYA:** Wait!

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou stops. He lifts the drill away from the shelf and sets it down on the bench, bit up.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Whoa.

**MAYA:** Don't fix the shelf.

**GRAMPS LOU:** How come?

[VISUAL: HOLD. Her mouth is open again. Same shape as the Scene 2 pause — and this time nothing comes out either, not yet. No nose scrunch.]

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou doesn't lean in and doesn't wait her out. He picks the rag up off the bench and wipes glue off his fingers, one finger at a time, eyes on his own hands. Keep the frame wide and easy — nothing here is a trap.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** You want to tell me what's going on?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Or you want to keep sorting. Both are fine with me.

**MAYA:** Can I say a thing and you don't get mad?

**GRAMPS LOU:** You can say a thing.

[VISUAL: She lets a breath all the way out. Her shoulders finally come down.]

**MAYA:** The shelf's fine. It didn't just fall.

**GRAMPS LOU:** No?

**MAYA:** I wanted to bring you your tea. Like a real job. A big one.

**MAYA:** So I got up on the crate and I got two fingers on it, and —

[VISUAL: She opens her hand flat and tips it over, the way the cup went.]

**MAYA:** I dropped it. It was me. It was me the whole time and I said it wasn't.

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou sets the folded rag down on the bench. That is all he does. No sigh. No head shake.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Thank you for telling me.

**MAYA:** Are you mad?

**GRAMPS LOU:** About the cup? A little.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Not at you.

**MAYA:** I'm sorry.

**GRAMPS LOU:** I know.

[VISUAL: Maya's shoulders are all the way down for the first time since the first frame of the episode. She takes one real breath — the first one that goes all the way in. **This is the relief beat. Give it air.**]

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou reaches up and rubs the two pencil dots off the front of the shelf with his thumb. Two seconds, no dialogue, no comment. The shelf is exactly as it was.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** So. What do we do about it?

## SCENE 5 — FIX-IT STAND, THE WORKBENCH END, CONTINUOUS
*(the visible, concrete repair — and Maya gets there herself)*

[VISUAL: Maya goes straight for the shelf. She has to get up on the crate again to reach the box. Gramps Lou puts one hand on the crate to steady it and says nothing about it.]

[VISUAL: She brings the box down and tips it out onto a clean rag on the bench. Four pieces.]

**MAYA:** There's still a hole. The handle.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Mm. Where's the last place it could be?

**MAYA:** I don't —

[VISUAL: She stops. She looks at the crate, then up at the shelf, then traces the way the cup came down with her eyes.]

**MAYA:** I was up on the crate. It went that way.

[VISUAL: She opens her hand flat and tips it over — the same gesture she used to tell the truth, working now as a way to find something. Because she said what really happened, she knows where to look.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Then go look that way.

[VISUAL: Maya flat on her stomach on the concrete with her head under the bench — static wide, two legs sticking out, one shoe untied. No flashlight, no cut-in. Hold the wide.]

**MAYA:** *(muffled)* I see it! It's by the wheel!

[VISUAL: She backs out with the handle in her fist and a stripe of dust across one cheek. She scrubs the dust off with her sleeve — continuity: the cheek is clean from this shot on.]

[VISUAL: She lines all five pieces up on the rag, biggest to smallest, without anybody asking her to — the same way she does the bolt jars. She is good at this and the shot should let us see that she is.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Count them. All five?

**MAYA:** All five.

**MAYA:** Gramps Lou. Can we do the chair thing?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Which chair thing?

**MAYA:** Glue does the sticking. Band does the holding.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Huh. Show me.

[VISUAL: He opens the third drawer down and sets a small tube on the rag instead of the big yellow bottle he used on the chair.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Not that one, though. Clay needs its own.

**MAYA:** Oh.

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou hands her a dry paintbrush.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** Brush the edges first. Glue won't stick to sawdust.

[VISUAL: Maya works the dry brush along every broken edge, one at a time, very seriously. Their hands work at the front edge of the bench, near camera, so the careful work reads without cutting in.]

[VISUAL: THE REPAIR — one long static wide, mostly wordless. Maya paints glue along one edge, thin, the way she watched him do the rung. Gramps Lou holds two pieces together with his big thumbs while she does the next one. No montage speed-up, no music swell — this is slow work and the shot is honest about it.]

[VISUAL: She presses two pieces together. They slide out of line. The seam gapes open and glue comes up on her thumb.]

**MAYA:** It moved!

**GRAMPS LOU:** It does that. Wipe it. We'll go again.

[VISUAL: Both edges wiped clean. Fresh thin line. This time Maya plants both elbows on the bench first.]

**MAYA:** Okay. Okay, I got it.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Hold still. Twenty seconds.

[VISUAL: Hold on the two of them holding one cup shut. Four hands, one cup, nobody talking.]

**MAYA:** There's a line on this one already.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Yep. Glued that one a long time ago.

**MAYA:** Who broke it?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Me.

**MAYA:** You?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Dropped it in the sink.

**MAYA:** Did you tell somebody?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Took me till dinner.

[VISUAL: Maya smiles. First smile since the first frame of the episode. Animate the old glue line on the handle as one clear pale stripe, the same weight as the blue stripe on the body, so it reads in the wide.]

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou cuts a red rubber band, loops it twice around the mended cup, and lets it snap tight. He stands it in the sun at the end of the bench, right next to the drying chair. Wide shot: a mended chair and a mending cup, side by side in the same patch of sun.]

**MAYA:** Is it fixed?

**GRAMPS LOU:** It's holding. That's not the same thing yet.

**MAYA:** When's it fixed?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Two hours.

---

## SCENE 6 — FIX-IT STAND, THE WORKBENCH END, AFTERNOON
*(the skill named, in one plain sentence, by a kid — not narrated)*

[VISUAL: The light has gone flat and warm. The mended cup stands in the sun with the red band still around it. Above the bench, the high shelf: no lip, no screws, no holes, no pencil marks — exactly the way it was all along. The cut strip of wood lies on the bench, unused, and Gramps Lou is sanding it smooth anyway, long even strokes. Maya is fixing the bolt jars — moving the small bolts out of the big jar one at a time — and eating the warm peach at last.]

**GRAMPS LOU:** That's two hours. Go on.

[VISUAL: Maya slides the red band off the cup and coils it on the bench beside it. She takes her hands away. The cup stands up on its own. Every seam shows as a thin pale line and the handle sits a hair crooked.]

**MAYA:** It's crooked.

**GRAMPS LOU:** A little.

**MAYA:** And you can see every line.

**GRAMPS LOU:** You can. Does it hold together?

[VISUAL: Maya puts one finger on the rim and presses, very careful. It holds.]

**MAYA:** Yeah. It holds.

**GRAMPS LOU:** Then it's a cup again.

**MAYA:** For tea?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Nope. It'll hold pencils just fine.

[VISUAL: He takes the flat carpenter pencil from behind his ear — the same one she said no to this morning — and holds it out to her, blunt end first. She takes it this time and stands it up in the mended cup. It stays.]

**MAYA:** Gramps Lou?

**GRAMPS LOU:** Mm.

**MAYA:** I didn't like today very much.

**GRAMPS LOU:** No?

**MAYA:** The bad part wasn't the cup breaking. The bad part was all the after.

**GRAMPS LOU:** What's the after part?

**MAYA:** Sorting was boring. And the peach got warm. And I looked at that shelf about a hundred times.

[VISUAL: A small bolt goes into the small jar. Clink. She's watching the jars now. She hasn't looked up at the shelf in a while, and the shot quietly lets us notice that.]

**MAYA:** Telling the truth was hard for one minute, but the lie was hard all day.

[VISUAL: Gramps Lou keeps sanding. He nods once. He does not add anything to it, does not repeat it back, does not explain it. The line is hers and it stays hers.]

**MAYA:** Tomorrow, can I bring you your tea? In a regular cup. Off the low shelf.

**GRAMPS LOU:** I'd like that.

---

## CLOSING — Your Turn

[VISUAL: Static wide of the whole stand. Maya sits on the upside-down crate out front, feet swinging, the mended cup in both hands with the pencil standing in it. Gramps Lou is at the far edge of the frame, still sanding, not looking at the camera and not talking. Maya looks up at us the way you'd look at a friend across the street. This is the only time in the episode that anybody does.]

**MAYA:** *(to the viewer — warm, easy, an invitation, not a lesson)* Your turn. Pick one small true thing today. Like "that was me." Or "I forgot." Say it out loud, even if it's a little embarrassing. Then you don't carry it all day like I did.

[VISUAL: She taps the mended cup once with one finger. It holds. She grins her gap-tooth grin.]

**MAYA:** See you on Maple Row.

[VISUAL: END CARD — Maple Row logo, warm daytime palette. On-screen text, one line: "Your turn: say one small true thing out loud today." No links, no subscribe ask aimed at the kid viewer, no next-episode tease. The card then clears onto SLOW PUSH-IN (3 of 3) on the mended cup in the sun, red band coiled beside it, one pencil standing in it. **The last frame of the episode is the seam** — the pale line running down the side of the cup, unmistakable. No motion except the push. Hold two seconds and out.]

### Never trimmed

- **The Scene 2 pause**, including the ~9 seconds around the nose scrunch. That shot is the episode and it is the Shorts cut.
- The first frame (the broken cup) and the near-silence that follows it.
- Maya's "Wait!" and the drill coming off the shelf. This is the only external clock in the episode.
- "Are you mad?" / "About the cup? A little." / "Not at you."
- The relief breath in Scene 4, and the thumb rubbing the pencil dots off the shelf.
- Maya's motive: "I wanted to bring you your tea. Like a real job. A big one."
- The failed first press in Scene 5 ("It moved!"). Without it the repair reads as luck instead of work.
- Gramps Lou's own confession ("Me." / "Dropped it in the sink." / "Took me till dinner.").
- Scene 6's three-item list ("Sorting was boring. And the peach got warm. And I looked at that shelf about a hundred times") and the skill line straight after it. Without the list, the skill line becomes a maxim.
- Gramps Lou's single nod after the skill line, and his silence after that.

**Per-character spoken split (for the voice session):** Maya 326 words, Gramps Lou 260. Maya carries the episode and speaks the only lesson line. Gramps Lou's longest single line is 11 words; every one of his lines is a question, a short answer, or a one-line instruction. No monologue anywhere. He has a visible physical task in all six scenes and the closing: carrying and sanding a board, gluing the chair rung, stretching the band, winding a cord, sighting and sawing the strip, wiping glue off his fingers, steadying the crate, holding pieces together, cutting the band, sanding again.

**Object-continuity load, kept deliberately low:** one hero prop, four states (whole / four pieces + a missing handle / five pieces laid out / mended with visible seams). The cup carries a painted **blue stripe** and nothing finer — no motif, no lettering, no small painted detail that would drift between generations. The only other detail that must read in a wide is the round dust ring on the shelf, which is a flat circle.

**Location:** one plate throughout — Gramps Lou's fix-it stand — worked in three areas: front counter under the high shelf, the high shelf itself, the workbench end. The second plate allowance goes unused on purpose; the middle of the episode depends on Maya being able to glance up at that shelf.
