# Episode Brief — EP03: The Candy on the Counter

**Skill pillar:** 03 / Waiting — delayed gratification — kid-plain: "Wait for the better thing instead of grabbing the OK thing now"

**Logline:** Maya is told the candy jar on the counter is for after dinner, talks herself into one early, and finds out the wait is exactly as long as it was — except now there's nothing at the end of it.

**Hook (first 15 seconds):** Frame one is the jar. Static wide on the kitchen counter: a jar with a wooden screw lid, eight wrapped candies inside — four red, four blue — and Maya stretched along the counter, **feet flat on the floor**, heels coming up, one arm all the way out and two fingers on the lid, turning it. The first sound in the episode is the lid ticking around. Gramps Lou is **in the same wide**, back to camera, hands in the dishwater, and says without turning around: "After dinner." Her hand goes flat on the lid and stays there. "How long?" No fade-in, no title card, no establishing shot of the house, nobody explaining anything first. The problem — a thing she wants, in reach, with a wait in front of it — is on screen before a single line of setup.

**This opening is staged deliberately *against* EP01's, which it would otherwise rhyme with almost exactly.** EP01's frame one is Maya up on the crate, arm raised to a high shelf, with Gramps Lou out of frame and audible at a task he can't hear over. Two episodes later that is a format, not an echo — a parent watching in order would see the same shot twice. So here: she is on the floor reaching *across* instead of climbing to reach *up*; he is *in* the wide instead of behind it, and he answers her instantly instead of missing everything; and **the step stool is held back to Scene 2**, where it becomes a rung of the staircase rather than the opening pose.

**Conflict:** The candy is eight inches from her hand and dinner is not a number. Maya asks how long "after dinner" is and nobody can tell her: the cornbread is in the oven and it's done when the top goes brown, and Gramps Lou doesn't know when that is either ("It does its own thing"). **There is no clock in this episode. No timer, no countdown, no numbers on a wall** — the only clock is the color of the cornbread through the oven window, pale yellow at the top of the show. Nell, 5, is staying over, and she is waiting badly and out loud in the same room.

**Wrong solution (the failed try):** Maya never decides to sneak one. She gets there down a staircase, and every step is a step that isn't a decision yet. First she tries to wait *properly* — crouched at the oven window, watching it, which is the worst waiting strategy there is and completely real. That's her fast fix, and it's this episode's version of balancing the two cup pieces back on the shelf in ep01: it lasts about eight seconds and makes the wait worse. Then she drags the step stool over — two hands are needed to hold a jar and work a lid, and fetching the stool is itself a rung she does not count as a decision — and takes one candy out because "I'm just going to hold it," and holds it in her fist for a whole scene. Then "I'm just going to see which one it is." Then the wrapper is off, and you can't un-take a wrapper off. The nose-scrunch tell fires in the half-second before it goes in her mouth. Her honest reason, said in her own words two scenes later: "I didn't think it was ever going to be dinner."

It fails on its own terms, immediately and visibly, with no adult involved: she ate it standing up, fast, watching the door — and the very next shot is the oven window, where the cornbread is exactly as pale as it was. **Nothing moved. Sneaking the candy did not shorten the wait by one second, and now she has nothing coming at the end of it.** Nell does the rest with one question: "Was it good?" Maya has to actually think about it, and can't answer.

**Right solution (the new try):** Two things get her there, and neither one is a grown-up explaining something.

*The mirror.* Nell is now doing precisely what Maya did an hour ago — crouched at the oven door, asking "is it brown," gripping the rabbit tighter every time the answer is no. Maya can't see her own waiting from the inside. She can see Nell's from the outside, and watching it is unbearable. This is the whole reason a second kid is in the episode.

*The nudge.* Nell asks "Is it brown now?" for the fourth time and Gramps Lou, hands in the dishwater, says: "Ask me when the table's set." Seven words. A chore, not a lesson. He does not say why, and he never says the word *wait*.

Maya is the one who turns a chore into a method, and she says her rule out loud so it's borrowable: **"Don't look at the oven till the forks are done."** Then she proves it's a method and not a fluke by using it a second time herself, unprompted, when Nell breaks and bolts back to the oven anyway: "Cups next. Then you can look." The table fills up — three plates, three forks, three cups, napkins — and Nell pulls up a fourth chair and folds a napkin for her rabbit, which is her own small win and is not the joke of the scene. The wait is not magicked away. It stays long, it has a dead patch in the middle, and the episode is honest about that. It's just survivable. By the time they look up, it's brown, and Nell is the one who gets to say so.

**The repair — an eaten candy cannot be un-eaten, so here is what the repair actually is.** After dinner Gramps Lou sets the jar on the table: "One each." Nell takes hers — seven goes to six. Maya's hand goes out for the jar, the exact reach from frame one, and stops. "I already had mine." Gramps Lou says there's plenty and doesn't argue when she says no; nobody makes her go without, and nobody praises her for it either. She screws the lid back on, puts the jar back on the counter with six in it, and Nell counts it out loud: "Six." **The count is the repair.** She can't un-eat the early one, so she doesn't get to eat the same candy twice — she takes the wait she skipped instead of spending it in two places. It is visible (a number a five-year-old reads off the counter from across the room, in the wide), concrete (a lid going back on), chosen by her rather than imposed on her, and it is not an apology. Her reason isn't guilt, either: Nell asks why and Maya says, "Because I didn't even taste that one." The one still in the jar is hers, she knows exactly where it is, and it'll be worth more tomorrow than the one she swallowed standing up.

**Skill named in kid language:** "Waiting's way easier when my hands have a job." — Maya, Scene 6, at the table, right after the lid goes back on. She sets it up off a short list of what she actually did, not off a maxim: "We did the plates, and the forks, and the cups. And then it was brown." Gramps Lou is drying a pan. He nods and adds nothing — no repeat, no improvement, no "that's right." The episode's other true sentence — "I didn't even taste that one" — lands two scenes earlier as a plain observation and is **never** promoted into a second moral. One skill, one line, one kid.

**20-second practice for the viewer:** Pick one small wait today and notice how it feels before and after. 40–44 words, 0:22, Maya at the kitchen table with the jar behind her on the counter — the only look at camera in the episode. She hands over two borrowable pieces, the way she handed over "that was me" and "I forgot" in ep01: (1) notice how the wait feels *before* it starts, (2) give your hands one job until it's time, then check how it feels *after*. Examples in kid units — a snack, a turn, a grown-up on the phone. No homework, no "comment below," nothing the kid is asked to report back. Sign-off stays series-standard: "See you on Maple Row."

**Characters in this episode:** Maya (7), Nell (5), Gramps Lou — all three to their locked sheets in `/characters/`. No one-off side character, no Theo, no fourth person at the table (the fourth chair is the rabbit's). Nell's rabbit is in frame in every shot she's in, is gripped tighter at her worst waiting moment, and gets its own chair at the finished table. Gramps Lou is at Maya's house, which the backlog already establishes (ep35 puts Maya home with him) — he exists here to set one rule and make one dinner, nothing else. Visible physical task in every scene: greasing and filling the pan, sliding it in, washing a stack of dishes, wiping the counter, drying, carrying the hot pan out with two mitts. His longest line is seven words. He never catches Maya, never scolds, never explains the lesson, and is allowed not to know something — he genuinely cannot say when the cornbread will be done.

**He also never finds out about the early candy, and the script has to keep it that way.** The Scene 3 exchange is between the two girls with the tap running; at the table, "I already had mine" is a *reason for declining*, not a confession — he takes it at face value, does not ask what she means, does not react, and the beat moves on. The second he registers it, Scene 6 turns into a small owning-up scene and ep03 starts borrowing ep01's pillar three weeks after ep01 aired.

**On the arithmetic: "One each" is said to the two girls**, and his hands stay in the dishwater through the whole beat. He must not take a candy on camera, and no line may draw attention to his not taking one. The episode asks a six-year-old to follow exactly three numbers — eight in the jar, seven after Maya's early one, six after Nell's — and nothing else may come out of that jar on screen.

**Locations used:** Maya's house only — one plate, worked in two areas held in the same wide (the kitchen counter with the oven and the jar; the kitchen table about six feet away). The second-plate allowance goes unused on purpose: the jar has to stay visible behind the kids the entire time they're setting the table, the same way the dust ring stayed visible in ep01. **This brief commissions the Maya's-house kitchen plate** — build the counter wall, the oven and the table as one wide. ep09 ("The Missing Cookie") is set in this exact wide and reuses it whole; ep19, ep28, ep35 and ep40 are elsewhere in the same house (a blanket fort, a bedroom door, the front door, a morning hallway) and reuse the plate's palette, wall treatment and floor, not this framing. Budget the kitchen wide against ep03 and ep09, not against all six.

**Continuity with ep01 — the mended cup does not appear here.** It lives at the fix-it stand holding pencils; that is where ep01 left it, on its last frame, and where ep04, ep21 and ep32 should find it. It will be tempting to dress it onto this counter as a callback because Gramps Lou is in the room — don't. A second small container of countable objects standing next to the candy jar is the one piece of set dressing that could actually break this episode's repair.

**Target runtime:** 6 min (honest budget 6:10 — see the scene map) · **Complexity:** S

## The two hard problems, named and solved

### 1. An eaten candy can't be un-eaten — and every mistake in this show gets a visible repair

Rejected, with reasons, so nobody re-opens these at 11pm:

- **She confesses and it becomes a truth episode.** That's ep01, and ep09 ("The Missing Cookie") is already the crumbs-on-the-shirt version of exactly this situation. **There is no lie anywhere in ep03.** Maya's truth costs four seconds, is spoken to a five-year-old who wasn't accusing her of anything, and the story keeps moving. Keeping the honesty bill that small is what keeps the two pillars distinct.
- **An adult finds out and imposes "no dessert."** That's a punishment, not a repair; it hands the lesson to a grown-up and turns a low-anxiety watch into a small scare.
- **She replaces it from somewhere else** — a pocket, a party bag, a friend. That's a lucky break, and this show doesn't have those.
- **She apologizes and the jar is never mentioned again.** That is the apology-and-cut-to-black the BIBLE bans by name.

Chosen: **she doesn't spend the same candy twice.** The early one came out of her own after-dinner share, so the repair is that she takes the wait she skipped — she reaches, stops, says "I already had mine," and puts the lid back on a jar with six in it while Nell says the number out loud. It's an action, it's hers, it's countable on screen, and it's the pillar rather than a detour into a different one. It also quietly seeds the "save it for later" muscle that ep06 and ep21 build on, without stealing either.

### 2. Waiting is internal, and internal is expensive in flat cutout with a static camera

Six physical carriers. Nothing in this episode asks the animation to show a feeling.

1. **The jar and the count — 8 → 7 → 6.** Flat solid circles, two colors, no lettering, no swirl, no highlight, countable in a wide, and counted out loud by a five-year-old. This is the dust ring and the miscounted bolt jars in one prop: the state of the wait *and* the ledger of the mistake. **The jar itself is drawn flat, not as glass** — one soft-outlined container shape in a pale tint, a plain wood-tone disc for the lid, and no highlight, reflection, refraction or rim-shine anywhere on it. Transparency is the least stable thing you can ask of AI-generated frames, and this is the one prop in the episode whose contents have to read exactly. The eight candy positions are a **locked layout**, a fixed two-row arrangement decided once; a candy leaving the jar means one circle switches off in place. Nothing rearranges, nothing settles, and the jar is never drawn from a new angle.
2. **The cornbread through the oven window.** Three states only — pale / edges going / brown. The single clock in the episode, readable by a six-year-old, and nothing anybody can hurry.
3. **The step stool.** The physical gate between Maya and the jar — the reach has to be a decision with a body in it. **It is not in frame one** (see the hook note: that staging belongs to ep01's crate and is not repeated here). It arrives in Scene 2, dragged over, and its arrival is one of the rungs. It stays in the wide from then on, unused, through the whole second half.
4. **One red wrapper on the counter.** Small, flat, persistent, and — importantly — **not hidden.** She didn't plan past the eating. It sits there through three scenes and goes in the bin with the same hand that puts the lid back on.
5. **The table filling up.** Each object placed is a chunk of wait visibly spent: plates, forks, cups, napkins, the rabbit's chair. **This is deliberately the opposite of the ep01 peach** — objects leaving her hands, not one going warm in them. The peach is not reused, and no held-object-that-spoils appears anywhere in this episode. **Build it as a locked additive layout, the same trick ep02 uses for the block tower:** thirteen flat shapes — three plates, three forks, three cups, three napkins, the rabbit's folded napkin — each a single flat silhouette dropped into a position fixed once on the plate, so every state of the table (bare → part-set → set → cleared) composites onto the one kitchen wide instead of costing a new generation. Nothing on the table carries a pattern, a rim, a print or a handle detail. If a place setting ever has to be *redrawn* rather than switched on, the layout wasn't locked properly and the episode has quietly stopped being an S.
6. **Maya's hand.** Reaching and stopping. Frame one, and the last beat before the closing. Same gesture, two completely different reasons.

Nell carries a seventh: she is the wait made *audible*, so the camera never has to go looking inside Maya.

### Staying out of ep02's way — the episode immediately before this one

ep02 and ep03 air back to back, and both hand a kid a dull physical job to get through a dull middle. A parent watching two Saturdays running could come away with one lesson instead of two, so the line has to be held in the writing:

- **In ep02 the boring work *is* the thing.** Crossing every block is what makes the tower stand; stop doing it and it falls. That lesson is about the work itself — finish the unglamorous middle of the thing you are making.
- **In ep03 the job has nothing to do with what she's waiting for.** Forks do not cook cornbread. Setting the table doesn't move the oven by one second, and the episode has *already proved* nothing Maya does can — that is exactly what the unchanged oven window right after the stolen candy is for. The job is somewhere to put her hands while a wait she cannot shorten goes past.

**"Nothing moved" is ep03's spine, not the chore.** The discovery is that taking the OK thing now bought her zero of the wait and cost her the better thing at the end of it. If the script starts selling the table-setting as a clever trick that makes the wait shorter, it has become ep02 with a kitchen in it. The wait stays exactly as long as it was, dead patch and all.

### Staying out of ep15's and ep16's way

The other two waiting episodes are already spoken for. ep15 ("The Swing Line") is waiting in public with other people and fairness pressure. ep16 ("The Present Under the Tree") is a days-long wait whose right solution is *making the waiting itself fun*. ep03's method is neither: **give your hands a job that has an end, and set a rule for when you're allowed to check.** If the script starts drifting toward "make the wait fun" or toward whose turn it is, it is writing a different episode.

## Why Nell is in this one

She is, and it isn't a cost decision dressed up as a story one.

- **She makes Maya's problem visible from outside.** A seven-year-old's wait happens behind her face. A five-year-old's happens on the floor, out loud, at the oven door. Maya learns by watching Nell do the exact thing she did an hour earlier — that's the pivot of the episode and it cannot be staged with Maya alone.
- **She removes the need for an interrogation.** Nell counts seven and says so, flatly, with no accusation in it, because she's five and counting is her favorite thing. Maya says "I had one." Two lines. No detective scene, no adult, no lie — which is exactly how ep03 avoids becoming ep01 again.
- **She gives Maya a job in the second half.** Helping somebody else wait is how a seven-year-old actually gets through her own wait, it's a behavior a parent will recognize instantly, and it needs no props.
- **She makes the repair legible.** The count is the repair, and she's the one who says the number.
- **Cost:** a locked character, no new design, no new plate, no new props except the rabbit, which is already locked. Her only new production cost is her voice.

Handled to her sheet: her waiting is played straight, never as comic relief — "I can't do it any more" is real and gets a beat, not a punchline. The rabbit is in every shot she's in and is gripped tighter at her worst moment. She is not fixed instantly: Maya's first job-offer doesn't hold her, the second one does, and she gets a genuine win of her own (the rabbit's chair, and being the one who announces "Brown!").

**Her counting stays on the two ledger moments only — "seven" in Scene 3 and "six" in Scene 6.** She does not count the plates, the forks or the cups onto the table as they go down. ep02 airs the week before and its entire third act is a child counting a job out loud as it's done; if Nell does the same thing here, two consecutive episodes share an audible spine and the count stops being ep03's repair and starts being the series' verbal tic.
