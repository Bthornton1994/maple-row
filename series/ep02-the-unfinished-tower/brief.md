# Episode Brief — EP02: The Unfinished Tower

**Skill pillar:** 02 / Finishing a hard thing — kid-plain: "Keep going after the fun part is over"

**Logline:** Maya and Theo's block tower falls twice in the exact same place, they both quit for opposite reasons, and the only way back is Theo's notebook plus Maya's hands — because the bottom seven layers never fell down at all.

**Hook (first 15 seconds):** Frame one is the tower going over — not the aftermath, the fall itself, already in motion before the video has a first frame to spare. Static wide on a square concrete paving slab set in the grass of Theo's back yard. The top half of a wooden block tower is tipping; Maya's hands are still up where it was; Theo is half a step back with a pencil in his fist. The first sound of the episode is blocks landing on grass — soft and dull, not a crash. **The frame is composed so the bottom of the tower stays standing while the top scatters.** Seven layers hold.

**The surviving bottom is planted twice, and only the second one stays up.** The audience sees it in frame one — and then Theo sweeps it flat inside the first minute to start clean, with nobody remarking on it either way. It happens again, identically, at the second fall, and *this* time Theo's hand stops before it sweeps, so those seven layers stand in every wide for the next forty-five seconds while both kids sit with their backs to them. Maya is the first person in the episode to say it out loud, at about 3:25. The audience has had the answer in frame since the first second and has watched it survive twice; that gap is the whole pleasure of the middle of this episode.

**Conflict:** The tower keeps falling, and worse than that, building it has stopped being fun. The bottom of the tower is careful work — blocks laid crossways, two per layer, turned a quarter each time, the way Theo's plan says. Somewhere around layer seven the careful part gets slow and dull and they start stacking single blocks straight up, fast, because they want it *tall*. It falls at layer eight. They sweep it all down and start over from zero. It falls at layer eight again. Nothing external ever touches it — no wind, no bump, no dog, no uneven ground. **The slab is flat and hard on purpose so the audience can never blame the floor.** The tower falls at the exact point where they stopped doing the boring thing, both times, and that is the only cause there is.

**Wrong solution (the failed try):** They quit, and they quit *differently* — that difference is the episode's engine and the reason it needs two kids.

- **Maya's quit** is the backlog's failed try, and it is fast, bright, and does not look like quitting at all. After the second fall she is up and across the yard inside ten seconds, sitting on the path with the chalk, drawing a big sun. She is not sulking, she has not decided anything, and she would be honestly surprised to hear she gave up. It fails because it is a loop, not an exit: the chalk sun takes ten seconds to outline — the fun part — and then has to be coloured in, a hundred small scribbles, and she slows to a stop on that too. She is now halfway out of two things instead of one.
- **Theo's quit** is slow, heavy, silent, and looks exactly like what it is. He reaches to sweep the bottom layers down to start over a third time — and stops, hand out over the blocks. He closes the notebook, puts it in his back pocket, and sits on the back step. His plan failed twice, so the plan is worthless, so there is nothing to do. It fails because he is right about the count and wrong about what it means.

**His stopped hand is what saves them, and it is a choice, not luck.** Because Theo quit *before* he swept, the careful bottom seven layers are still standing on the slab for the whole low middle of the episode, in every wide, behind both of them.

**Right solution (the new try):** Maya comes back first, because the hero's arc is hers — and she comes back for a Maya reason, not a virtue. Bored of the chalk, sitting on the path looking at nothing, she sees what has been in frame since the first second: the tower is not gone. Most of it is still up. "Theo. It's still standing."

He says no. He is short and quiet, not unkind: *"I already tried my best plan. Twice."* She does not argue with him and she does not touch his notebook — she asks him a question only he can answer, and she actually wants to know. *"How many did we get? Before it fell."* He knows. He takes the notebook out himself, of his own accord, flips back two pages, and there are two drawings in it, one per fall.

**Then each of them supplies exactly one half of the answer, and neither half is enough alone:**

| | What they bring | Why the other one can't |
|---|---|---|
| **Theo** | **The where.** Two drawings, same height. "Seven. Both times." He has the record, the count, and the pattern. | Maya was not writing anything down and could not tell you how tall it got either time. |
| **Maya** | **The what.** Down on her knees at the surviving stack with both hands on it: "These ones are turned." She turns one flat to show it. "The top ones we just went straight up." | Theo has not touched the blocks since he quit and will not until the answer is guaranteed. |

Theo's plan was never wrong — his own page says *turn every one*, with a crossed-blocks drawing and the number **20**. They abandoned his plan at layer eight, twice. Maya is the one who says so out loud: *"Your plan was fine at the bottom."*

Knowing the answer still does not get Theo's hands on a block, because his problem was never information — it is that he will not risk being wrong a third time. So Maya does not reassure him and does not promise him anything:

> **MAYA:** Let's do it again.
> **THEO:** It'll fall again.
> **MAYA:** Probably.
> *(beat)*
> **MAYA:** Count for me.

She starts without him and gives him a job that risks nothing. He counts. Somewhere around eleven there is a block in his hand and he did not notice picking it up. They build up from the seven that never fell — **they continue it, they do not restart it** — crossing every layer, slow, boring, counted out loud, no montage and no speed-up. At sixteen it wobbles badly; Maya's hand shoots out and **steadies it — she touches it, and it does not right itself**, because nothing in this episode is allowed to resolve on its own; Theo says the next number; no music sting, no slow motion, nobody screams. At twenty they stop, because twenty was the number on the page. Theo closes the notebook. **Same gesture as his quit, forty seconds of screen time apart, meaning the exact opposite — animate it identically and let the audience do the work.**

**Skill named in kid language:** *"It fell at the boring part, not the hard part."* — Maya, Scene 7, sitting on the back step with the finished tower still standing on the slab. Like the pilot, it lands right after a short concrete list of the actual day ("The fun part was the bottom. Then it got boring. Then we both went and did something else"), so it reads as a thing she noticed rather than a maxim. **Theo does not repeat it, improve it, or agree with it out loud.** He is writing in the notebook. The line is hers and it stays hers.

**20-second practice for the viewer:** From the backlog row: *pick one thing you stopped this week and try it one more time.* Maya delivers it to camera — the only fourth-wall look in the episode, warm, an invitation, not a lesson — and hands the viewer the concrete version of the episode's actual discovery: **don't start it over, go find the place where you stopped and do one more piece.** ~40 words, 0:22, sitting on the step beside the half-coloured chalk sun. Nobody mentions the sun and she does not finish it on camera — but **the last frame of the episode is her hand picking the yellow chalk back up**, with the finished tower standing behind her, no line over it. It is the practice made literal, it costs nothing (existing prop, same wide, push-in 3 is a scale-up of that frame), and it deliberately gives EP02 a different closing image from EP01's slow push onto the seam in the mended cup. **Do not close this one on the tower.** Two of the first three episodes already end on a slow push onto the hero object wearing its scar; a third in a row turns the show's best closing move into a template. Signs off on the series line, "See you on Maple Row."

**Characters in this episode:** **Maya** (7) and **Theo** (8), both to their locked sheets in `/characters/` — this is Theo's first appearance in the series and the episode is built to establish him by watching him work, never by explaining him (see "Establishing Theo" below). **No adult, and that is a decision** — no Gramps Lou, no Ms. Rivera, no off-screen parent voice, nobody calling from a house. If any adult asks the good question, the kids did not carry the episode, and the season needs proof they can before week 3. It is also the cheapest episode shape available: two voices, one plate, no third character sheet. **No one-off side character.** No Nell — a five-year-old in this yard either knocks the tower over (which is ep26, a different skill) or has to be managed through the whole silent middle.

**Locations used:** **Theo's yard next door** — one plate, no second plate, the whole episode. Chosen over Maya's house for one reason: the blocks are Theo's, in Theo's yard, built to Theo's plan, so quitting costs him something real without anybody having to say so. Worked in three areas, exactly as the pilot worked the fix-it stand: the **paving slab** in the grass (the build), the **concrete path** to the gate (Maya's chalk, still in the same wide as the slab), and the **back step** (Theo's quit, and the skill line). **Maya walks away without ever leaving the plate** — her quit and the abandoned tower stay in the same static wide, which is both cheaper and better. Plate dressing to build once and reuse for every later Theo's-yard episode: low fence across the back with a gate through to Maya's yard (establishes "next door" with no line of dialogue and is never used), two-step concrete back stoop at frame right, the paving slab centre, the path, a hose reel on the fence, one small tree at frame left whose shadow moves across the slab to carry time. No adults and no pets in this episode's dress.

**Target runtime:** 7 min · **Complexity:** S

## Establishing Theo without stopping the story

He is introduced the way the show introduces everything: by being in the middle of doing something when the video starts. **There is no introduction beat anywhere in this episode.** Nobody says "this is my friend Theo," nobody explains that he lives next door, nobody remarks on the notebook, the glasses, or the cowlick.

| Beat | What establishes him | What is NOT said |
|---|---|---|
| Frame one | He is already in the shot with a pencil in his fist and the notebook corner visible in his back pocket. Glasses, cowlick, green-and-white stripes, brown shorts — all locked, all just there. | No entrance, no reaction shot to camera, no name card. |
| First 20 seconds | The tower has just fallen and **he writes something down.** One gesture, and you know the whole boy. | Nobody asks him what he's writing. |
| Scene 1 | He sweeps the surviving seven layers flat to start clean. A tower with a broken top is not a tower to him. | Nobody argues with him about it. |
| Scene 1 | Maya says his name, once, in the middle of an ordinary sentence. He says hers once, later. | Never "Theo, my neighbour." |
| Scene 3 | **His quitting happens on screen, in full**, so the audience learns his flaw by watching it instead of being told: the hand stopped over the blocks, the notebook closed, the walk to the step. | He never announces that he is quitting. His longest word on the subject is "done." |
| Scene 5 | **He is right.** The count, the pattern, the page that already said the answer. His competence is the thing that turns the episode. | He is never the fussy one, never corrected, never the joke. |
| Throughout | He inherits the adult rule for visible physical tasks — Theo is never just standing and talking. Pencil, notebook, a block in his hand, sweeping, counting on his fingers, sitting with his elbows on his knees. | — |

**Guardrails from `/characters/friend.md`, restated because this is his debut and it sets the pattern for 38 more episodes:**

- **Never the butt of the joke for being careful.** The careful bottom is the part that survives. Twice.
- **Never mean when grumpy — short and quiet.** His refusals are one to five words: "No." "It'll fall again." "I'm done." He never snaps at Maya, never blames her for walking off, and never says the word "quit" about anybody.
- **Real problem-solving ideas even when frustrated.** His diagnosis arrives while he is still refusing to build. Being frustrated does not make him useless in this show.
- **He is not a sounding board.** Delete either kid from the third act and it does not work: without Theo nobody knows *where* it failed, without Maya nobody ever puts a hand on it again.
- **Nobody accuses anybody.** Maya never tells Theo he gave up; Theo never tells Maya she abandoned his blocks. They both just come back. Any line of blame turns this into ep30 or ep34, which are other episodes.
- **Maya never touches his notebook.** He takes it out himself, every time. ep20 ("My Diary, Not Yours") is the notebook-privacy episode with the roles reversed — do not spend it here by accident.

## Production notes the script and frames must honour

- **Camera:** static wide throughout, plus exactly **three slow push-ins**, all marked in the script — the silence after the second fall, the low middle in Scene 4 (time passing inside one unbroken shot), and the Closing. No close-ups, no cuts inside a scene, same grammar as the pilot.
- **The blocks:** plain unpainted wood-tone rectangles, all identical, one size, in a flat cardboard box on the grass. **No letters, no numbers, no painted faces** — alphabet blocks drift between AI generations and invite reading instead of watching. Twenty-four blocks total, which is the plan's twenty plus spares.
- **A taller tower is not a new key frame.** Tower states are additive stacks of one rectangle on one locked plate; like the pilot's push-ins, they cost nothing. Frame budget stays at the 20-key-frame ceiling with room to spare, and the collapsed count gets published with the shot list.
- **Hero prop, four states only:** whole and growing · fallen with the bottom seven standing · rebuilt past eight · finished at twenty with a chalk mark on layer eight.
- **Continuity with EP01 — the mended cup does not appear here, and must not be dressed in anywhere.** It lives at Gramps Lou's fix-it stand holding pencils; that is where EP01 left it and where EP04, EP21 and EP32 should find it. This episode never leaves Theo's yard and nobody carries it over. **Theo's pencil is Theo's own**, out of his own pocket with the notebook — never the flat carpenter pencil from behind Gramps Lou's ear, which is a fix-it-stand prop with a scene of its own in EP01.
- **The chalk mark** is Maya's, made with the yellow chalk still on her fingers while they build past eight, unexplained at the time. In Scene 7 she points at it: "That's where it kept falling." It is the show's repair-shows-its-seams motif carried forward from the mended cup — a different object, a different grammar (a mark she chose, not a scar the glue left), deliberately not the same beat.
- **No external cause, ever.** Nothing in the script, the sound design, or the animation may suggest wind, a bump, a slope, a soft patch of grass, or a bad block. The slab is flat and hard and stays in frame. The only cause is what the kids did.
- **Low-anxiety throughout.** Blocks land on grass, dull and soft. The wobble at sixteen gets no music sting, no speed change, no gasp. Nobody cries, nobody is in trouble, no adult is going to find out about anything.
- **Maya's nose-scrunch tell fires exactly once, and not the way it fired in the pilot.** Half a second, mid-motion, as she stands up out of the second fall and turns toward the chalk — no hold after it, no push-in on it, no silence around it. In EP01 the tell sat inside a long held pause because lying is a decision she has to make. Quitting is the opposite: it does not feel like a decision at all, and the staging has to say so. See open question 1.
