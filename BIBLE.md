# Show Bible

## Premise
On one ordinary street, four kids and one steady grown-up figure out — one small mistake at a time — how to choose the harder, better thing instead of the easy wrong one.

## Hero
**Maya, 7.** Quick, warm, a little impulsive. She reaches for the fast fix — hide it, fudge it, grab it — before she reaches for the right one. She wants to be trusted with bigger things, and the season's quiet arc is learning that trust gets built by what you do *after* something goes wrong, not by never messing up.

## Recurring cast, and the skill each one carries
| Character | Age | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Maya** | 7 | Hero — the season-long arc of slowing down before choosing |
| **Theo** | 8 | Next door neighbor — careful, plan-obsessed, quits the second something isn't perfect → finishing hard things |
| **Nell** | 5 | Maya's cousin, stays over often — big feelings, no filter yet → naming feelings, anger |
| **Gramps Lou** | adult | Runs the fix-it stand on the corner — patient, unhurried, answers questions with questions → money basics, steady problem-solving |
| **Ms. Rivera** | adult | Maya's teacher, lighter recurring role in school-set episodes → responsibility and safety at school |

All five have locked sheets in `/characters/` — `hero.md`, `friend.md`, `sibling-or-cousin.md`, `adult.md`, `teacher.md`.

One-off classmates and neighbors (like Sam in ep05, "Sam's Crayon") can appear for a single episode without becoming locked recurring characters — keeps the cast small without making the world feel empty.

## World rules — what never happens in this show
- No magic, gadget, or lucky break fixes the problem. Only a choice does.
- No villains. The conflict is always an ordinary kid situation, never a bad guy.
- No adult monologues. Adults ask a question; kids find the answer.
- Every mistake gets a visible, concrete repair — never just an apology and a cut to black.
- Nobody is laughed at or humiliated for the lesson to land.
- No cliffhangers, no scares. This is a low-anxiety watch, every time.

## Visual rules (built for AI-assisted, cheap, consistent animation)
- Flat, rounded cutout style — soft shapes, minimal fine detail that would break between AI-generated frames.
- Each character has **one locked outfit and one locked color set**, always. Never redesign mid-season — see `/characters/`.
- Four reusable location plates, built once and reused constantly: Maya's house, Theo's yard next door, Gramps Lou's fix-it stand, Ridgeline Elementary.
- Camera: static wide shots plus the occasional slow push-in. No handheld, no fast cutting.
- Warm, consistent daytime palette per location, so small inconsistencies between AI-generated frames are less noticeable.

## Voice rules
- Vocabulary a 7-year-old already owns. Short sentences. Real contractions.
- The lesson is named out loud by a kid, in one plain sentence — never narrated at them.
- Adults ask ("What else could you try?"), they don't lecture.
- No episode ends with a moral delivered straight to camera.

## Episode formula
**Hook → problem → failed try → new try → skill named in kid language → 20-second practice for the viewer to try at home.**

## What this show is NOT
- Not a rescue/action show — no Paw-Patrol-style mission structure.
- Not a Sunday-school sermon with a kid cast.
- Not adult humor wearing a kids' skin.
- Not a show where the takeaway is "be nice," with nothing concrete underneath.
