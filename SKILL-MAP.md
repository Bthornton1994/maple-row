# Skill Map

12 skill pillars, each mapped to kid-plain language and an age lens. The full table lives in `/trackers/skill-curriculum.csv` — this file is the reasoning behind it.

## How age-fit works
- **5–7:** the skill shows up as a single concrete moment — one cup, one crayon, one wait.
- **8–10:** the same skill shows up with a social complication attached — a friend is watching, the consequence is bigger, the shortcut is more tempting.

Most of the pilot season is written to the 5–7 end on purpose: easier to animate, easier to read, and 8–10 kids will still watch a good story even if the hero is a little younger than they are.

## The 12 pillars
1. Telling the truth
2. Finishing a hard thing
3. Waiting / delayed gratification
4. Asking for help
5. Sharing vs. keeping boundaries
6. Money — earn, save, spend, give
7. Names for feelings
8. What to do when angry
9. Being a good friend
10. Fixing a mistake
11. Stranger / online safety, at kid level
12. Taking care of your stuff

Full breakdown (kid-plain wording, why it matters to parents, age-fit examples, planned episode count per pillar) is in `/trackers/skill-curriculum.csv`.

## On the 40-episode backlog
`/trackers/episode-backlog.csv` distributes 40 episode ideas across these 12 pillars, weighted toward the pillars with more natural story variety (truth, effort, money, repairing mistakes get 4 each; the rest get 3). The backlog is organized by skill for curriculum clarity, but **production order should interleave skills** episode-to-episode — two "telling the truth" episodes back to back reads as repetitive even if they're weeks apart on the calendar. 90-DAY-PLAN.md handles this for the pilot season; re-shuffle the remaining backlog the same way before scheduling further out.

8 episodes in the backlog are flagged (`flag` column) as leaning too abstract or too socially complex for the 5–7 end of the audience — simplify the conflict before scripting those, or shift them later once the show has proven it can handle a harder topic well.
