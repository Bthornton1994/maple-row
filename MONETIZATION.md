# Monetization — Made for Kids

*YPP thresholds researched September 2026. Recheck before applying — YouTube has announced tier changes taking effect Feb 1, 2027 (see below).*

## Current YouTube Partner Program thresholds
- **Early access tier (fan funding only — no ad revenue):** 500 subscribers + 3,000 valid public watch hours in the trailing 12 months (or 3M Shorts views in 90 days). Unlocks Super Thanks, Channel Memberships, Shopping. Super Chat/Stickers/Memberships and comments are off on Made for Kids content regardless, so this tier's main relevance to us is later, non-kids-designated content if the channel ever expands.
- **Full monetization tier (ad revenue):** 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public watch hours in the trailing 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days). This is the one that matters for a Made for Kids channel — ad revenue via contextual ads.
- **Heads up:** thresholds for the full tier are set to roughly double on Feb 1, 2027 (reportedly 1,000 subs + 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views). If the 90-day plan's pace holds, this channel should clear the *current* threshold before that change — worth tracking watch hours weekly so there's no ambiguity about which threshold applies.
- Both tiers also require: no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-step verification enabled, and a linked AdSense account.

## What Made for Kids does to the money
Personalized ads are off — only contextual ads run, at roughly **$1–3 RPM** vs. $5–15 for general-audience content. This is structural, not a growing-pains phase — it doesn't improve much with scale. Plan around it rather than against it.

## Staged plan
**Before 1,000 subs**
- No merch push, no "buy my course for kids" — there's no audience yet to sell to, and pushing too early reads as thin.
- Focus entirely on the weekly habit (90-DAY-PLAN.md) and hitting the YPP watch-hour threshold.

**At YPP (full tier, ad revenue)**
- Set expectations low on ad RPM per the numbers above. Ad revenue on a kids channel is a floor, not the plan.
- Apply as soon as both the subscriber and watch-hour thresholds clear — don't wait for a round number.

**Parent-facing channels (later, once there's an audience)**
- An email list or simple site, addressed to parents explicitly — never framed as something for the child viewer to sign up for. No kid-facing signup, ever, on any channel.
- This is a genuine COPPA line, not just good practice — see YOUTUBE-KIDS-COMPLIANCE.md.

**Affiliates**
- Only products a parent would thank you for recommending: books, well-made simple games, genuinely useful learning tools.
- No junk toys, no impulse-purchase bait, nothing that trades on a kid nagging a parent after watching.

**Brand deals**
- What's acceptable in kids content is narrower than general content — no disguised ads inside an episode, no product placement that isn't clearly, separately labeled as sponsored, and nothing that would fail the "would a parent be glad their kid watched this" test.

**Product that actually fits, once the show has an audience**
- Printable skill cards (tie directly to the 12 pillars in SKILL-MAP.md)
- A parent guide — the "why it matters" column from `skill-curriculum.csv`, expanded
- A classroom pack, if teachers start using the show organically (this tends to happen with skill-focused kids content — worth watching for, not chasing)

## What this channel will not do
- No fake educational claims ("this is educational" is not a shield against COPPA or against bad content)
- No adult jokes hidden in kids' wrapping
- No gambling-adjacent mechanics (loot-box-style "surprise" framing, chance-based CTAs)
- No kid-facing signup or data collection, on any platform, ever
